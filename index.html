Newport Township · Luzerne County, Pennsylvania · as of Oct 7, 2026

Where the Newport Township data center would go

Newport Township sits on the southeast bank of the Susquehanna River at the lower (southwest) end of the Wyoming Valley, just west of Nanticoke. Glen Lyon lies in a narrow valley at the head of Newport Creek, between ridges. The project address is south of Glen Lyon at Lee, below Penobscot Mountain. Official map layers only: USGS basemaps and place names, U.S. Census address points, and PA DEP mining and boundary data.

The exact footprint is not public

No public document gives the 1,386-acre boundary or parcel numbers. The red pin is the one address officials have given (75 Main Road, Lee section, per the township building inspector in the Times Leader, Oct 5, 2026), placed by the U.S. Census geocoder. That point is interpolated along the road, not a parcel. The Times Leader ties that address to Newport Aggregate Inc.'s active quarry, which is the large pit visible on the aerial image about 0.6 mile north of the pin. The project's other 7 permitted buildings are in a mixed-use zone at an undisclosed location.

Reading the mining layer

The orange and blue shapes are DEP's Digitized Mined Areas: underground workings traced from the original company mine maps (many from the Blue Coal collection, digitized by EPCAMR and DEP). Each shape records the operator, colliery, coal seam and, where known, the date last mined. Orange marks workings by the Susquehanna Coal Co., which began mining at Glen Lyon in 1869, and its successor the Susquehanna Collieries Co. Shapes overlap because each seam was mined at a different depth.

Large parts of the area around the project address are undermined. The dashed purple outlines are DEP's abandoned-mine-land inventory: open strip pits, spoil piles, refuse banks and subsidence-prone ground, marked as abandoned or reclaimed.

Two historical maps can be laid over today's map from the layer menu: the 1894 USGS Shickshinny topographic sheet (surveyed 1890), and a Susquehanna Coal Co. surface map of the Glen Lyon Colliery from the Pennsylvania Mine Map Atlas. Use the opacity slider to compare them with the aerial image.

Company mine maps (Pennsylvania Mine Map Atlas, Penn State and PA DEP)

  • Mine Map Atlas viewer. About 135 scanned sheets cover Glen Lyon; 53 are tagged to the Susquehanna Coal Co., the Susquehanna Collieries Co., or the Glen Lyon Colliery.
  • Surface Map, Glen Lyon Colliery, Susquehanna Coal Co. (undated), sheet BLUE_WAN-0B-01-01: PDF · DEP catalog record. This is the overlay on the map above.
  • Surface Map, Glen Lyon Colliery, Susquehanna Collieries Co., dated Oct 6, 1930, sheet WBDO_059-01-0A: PDF · DEP record
  • Glen Lyon Colliery surface, sheet BMSA_1079-001: PDF · DEP record
  • Underground maps of "No. 6 Colliery" (Susquehanna Collieries), e.g. the 1935 Cooper Vein map: DEP record
  • Nanticoke: Surface Map, Susquehanna No. 7 Colliery, Susquehanna Coal Co.: DEP record

Geological survey sheets, 1884–1889 (Penn State Libraries, public domain)

  • Second Geological Survey, Northern Anthracite Field, Mine Sheet No. II, "Newport Sheet", Feb. 1887. Shows the Glen Lyon street grid, "No. 6 Colliery, Susquehanna Coal Co." with its shaft, company land lines, Wanamie and Lee.
  • Mine Sheet No. III, "Nanticoke", Jan. 1884: Susquehanna Coal Co. Nanticoke shafts and breakers.
  • Mine Sheet No. I, "Southwest of Nanticoke", 1887

Town plans (Sanborn fire insurance maps, Penn State Libraries)

  • Glen Lyon: June 1897 · March 1903 · Sept. 1907 · April 1928. Building-by-building plans of the company town.
  • Nanticoke: Aug. 1884 · Nov. 1920 · 1928, also covers Alden and Sheatown

USGS historical topographic maps

  • Shickshinny 15-minute sheet, surveyed 1890, dated 1894: GeoPDF. This is the 1894 overlay above. Reprints from 1897, 1901, 1910, 1922 and 1937 also exist.
  • Nanticoke 7.5-minute sheets from 1946, 1954 and 1976 are available through USGS topoView.

County atlas

  • 1873 D.G. Beers Atlas of Luzerne County, Newport Township plate (p. 121). It shows "Susquehanna Coal Co." holdings along Newport Creek. A low-resolution copy is on a volunteer genealogy site, and Wilkes University Archives holds a physical copy of the 1873 county map.
